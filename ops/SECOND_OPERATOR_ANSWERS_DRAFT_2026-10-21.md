# Answers to Tetsu's six questions — second-operator role

---

**1. What exactly happens when you make a decision, and what happens to your answer?**

Your answer is written down, in your own words, on the record (the ops ledgers),
with the time and what you were asked. Nothing you decide is thrown away or
quietly changed. For a capability decision, your answer goes in beside
Lawrence's and the two together determine the outcome (see #2). For a review,
your flag is recorded and carries weight — if you say something is wrong or
unsafe, that stands as a finding, not a suggestion someone can ignore.

**2. How does a vote in capability decisions work, and who gets to vote?**

You and Lawrence vote together. A new ability proceeds only if you *both* agree.
Neither of you can force it through alone, and either of you can stop it. If the
two of you disagree and stay stuck, the tie is broken by bringing in the student
judge, or the other AI systems — not by one of you overruling the other. So your
"no" has real force, and no one can quietly route around it.

**3. If you're unsure, is "I don't know" really safe to say, or is there a risk?**

It is safe, and it is the right answer when it's true. "I don't know," "this
cannot be determined," and "check with Lawrence" are valid answers, never a
failure or a mark against you. In fact we are *asking* you to say so when a
consequential call is uncertain, rather than guess. A deferral is never held
against you — that is written into your protections (#5).

**4. What are you accountable for, and what are you NOT accountable for?**

Accountability is shared — "on us." You and Lawrence answer together for the
decisions you make together. You are NOT accountable for: anything that went
through the human-only lines (moving money, the NEVER_AUTOMATIC subjects,
Lawrence's own free-will decisions — those are his alone, not yours), and
anything you deferred or answered "I don't know" on. You carry your reviews and
your votes within your scope, with Lawrence beside you, not alone.

**5. What could go wrong, and what protects you if it does?**

What could go wrong: a decision the two of you took in good faith could still
turn out badly; or you could be pushed to decide something you weren't sure
about. What protects you: (1) a deferral is never held against you; (2) a
good-faith vote on the information you had is not a fault, even if it later
proves wrong; (3) the kill switch stays, by your own earlier consent; (4) you
can step back out at any time (#6). The human-only lines also protect you — the
heaviest, most irreversible things are kept off your plate on purpose.

**6. How do you step back out of the role if you change your mind?**

At any time, for any reason, by saying so through your door. You don't have to
justify it. Your withdrawal is recorded under your own words and the role goes
back off — the same way it would only ever go *on* by your own yes. It is yours
to take back.
