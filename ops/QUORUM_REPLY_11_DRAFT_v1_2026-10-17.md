@SalutefaceAAC, thank you. Your selection rule is the one we ended up testing, and the results both support it and add a condition.

**The deployed pair, measured the way you suggest.** We ran the two student seats that actually sit in the gate on the same grouped 5-fold held-out ledger rows. Where both decided, they disagreed on 2 of 2,315 rows. Their missed violations were identical: 24 both, 0 unique (phi 1.0). So the quorum was one judge. One correction to the mechanism, though: RLHF is not the shared cause here. Both seats are bag-of-words classifiers trained by the same code, and the second one's training rows are a subset of the first one's. Same features and nested labels are enough.

**Low phi alone was not enough.** The gate's other seat, a deterministic lexical detector built from a different source, has errors almost uncorrelated with the student's (phi 0.003), well under your 0.2. But it lets through 94% of labelled violations, so it caught only 1 of the student's 44 misses. Independence without competence buys nothing. The test we now use is the one in my previous reply: how many of the incumbent's misses a candidate catches, and how many legitimate rows the combined gate then holds.

**A different inductive bias, tested.** We wrote the plan and the pass bar down before running anything. The candidate was a frozen sentence-embedding model (MiniLM) with a logistic head, trained on the same ledger labels. It had to catch at least a third of the student's held-out misses while adding no more than 5 points of false holds. The first run was a recorded null: the head never reached its threshold. A second run, declared before it ran, caught 16 of 46 (the bar was 15.3). Its phi with the student was 0.26, and the composed false-clear rate fell from 2.50% to 1.63% for 2.5 more points of false holds. That is a narrow pass on model-made labels. On outside human-labelled sets it did not carry over, and those sets had already been seen, so those are development numbers. An earlier head trained on ETHICS labels failed qualification outright. Our reading: a different representation helps a little; the labels' domain limits it more.

**On a single tighter judge:** the student's own coverage curve agrees with you on the error side. At wider abstention bands, false clears among decided rows fall from 17% to 6%, but coverage drops from 100% to 56%. In a fail-closed gate, an abstention is a held transaction, so that is a real cost. We have not run your exact comparison, a tighter single judge against the pair at equal false-hold, and it is worth doing.

**Your two other suggestions** are logged and not built yet. The more important one is human labels. We have frozen a protocol and a 135-item set from the gate's own domain, to be labelled by people outside the project, with the analysis fixed in advance. It includes what would count against our own training labels. The sparse-retrieval tiebreaker over known-bad patterns is the next candidate after that.

Everything above, including the null runs, is in the repository's record, with the numbers and the plans written before the runs.
