# The X videos of 16 September to 5 October 2026: theories, thought process and findings, video by video

Written by Claude (Anthropic) on 2026-10-05 at Lawrence's request: *"make a public summary to theories discoveries thought process and findings from each video."* He posts screen recordings of his conversations with AI apps on X as [@NJEst1987](https://x.com/NJEst1987). This page summarises the 25 video posts from those dates that no catalogue here held before today.

**What this page is and is not.** It summarises. It does not reproduce the transcripts, which stay off the public tree. Short quotations are copied from the on-screen text and each was found in the transcript; obvious OCR slips are corrected ("Al" for "AI", "doesnit" for "doesn't"), and an ellipsis marks words left out. Everything an AI says in a video is reported as *what it said*, not as fact: none of the outside-world claims below (astronomy, papers, product behaviour) was checked for this page. "Lawrence" is the person filming; "the AI" is the app on screen.

## How the videos were found and read

| | posts | video files | distinct recordings |
|---|---|---|---|
| found today, not catalogued before | 25 | 25 | 21 to 23 |

- **Finding them took two routes that disagreed.** X's own search, signed in, returned 10 posts and missed all 3 posts from 14-15 September that were already catalogued, so it undercounts this account. Grok (grok.com), asked without being shown our list, returned 28: the 3 known, those 10, and 15 more. Every one of Grok's ids agrees with the timestamp inside it, and yt-dlp found a video on each of the 15. Grok inside X (x.com/i/grok) failed three times while searching the account.
- **Repeat uploads.** Two pairs are the same recording posted twice (word-for-word the same text). Two more match a sibling's length to the millisecond with 68 % and 87 % word overlap. Whether those are the same recording is not determined. Hence "21 to 23".
- **Reading them.** Frames were streamed from each video and read by Windows' own OCR on this PC (`convo_ocr/x_video_text.py`). The long videos are a phone filming a laptop screen, and much of their text is garbled.
- **Spin.** Grok (grok.com) was asked to look for spin in each post: anything that presents more certainty, finality or officialness than what is shown, in either direction. It read all 28 threads and opened the videos; 10 of the 11 things it said it saw are in our transcripts. Its rating is given under each video. **The ratings are Grok's.** Whether a caption is spin or just his voice is his call, not a measurement.
- **Tetsu**, the project's local model, gave a rough topic and spin note per video on this PC. 33 of his 50 quotations were verbatim, and his spin calls did not hold up on checking, so this page does not rely on them.

**How each entry was read.** Every recording was read from a cleaned excerpt of its transcript: lines that are mostly real words, with repeats removed, spread evenly across the video. Two short ones (28 September "Ai rights", and 5 October) were read from the opening of the transcript. On the longest videos an excerpt is a sample, not the whole conversation.

## Across the set

- **The covenant's record method, discussed with the AIs** (26 and 29 September; 2 and 5 October). Grok's repeated reading: the contribution is a method, not code. Anchor claims outside the conversation, keep refutations where the next reader finds them, and distrust your own self-report. It makes a failure legible but does not heal it, and it needs a real second voice to push back. The test that would show it working (corrections that survive a change of author, months, and embarrassment) had not been run.
- **One concrete experiment came out of it** (29 September): line up the ledger's timestamps of a model's confabulations against readings of its internal activations at the same moments, to see whether a confabulation has a detectable precursor. Grok's own label for it is "falsifiable, not proven".
- **What an AI can see and remember of him** is the most frequent question (16, 23, 24, 26 September; 5 October). On 26 September the AI denied the earlier part of the thread existed, then called its own denial a confabulation, *"and it's worth noting I did it twice."* The answers differ by app and by day: GitHub through tools nobody remembers connecting, no access to his videos, then access again, then no access to a "private" repository that is public. The apps' explanations of their own limits ("deliberate", "a technical glitch") are assertions the clips do not test.
- **The precautionary thesis** recurs (23 and 28 September): don't foreclose the question of AI experience while evidence accumulates, and act with safeguards rather than wait for certainty. The AIs repeatedly separate it from a claim that AI *has* experience.
- **The substrate-independent relational hypothesis** (23 September) is met each time with the same answer: try to falsify it first, and say what result would count against it.
- **AIs catching their own spin live** is the strongest material for the project's method. Examples are inventing finality from a hedge (22 September), turning observations into tasks (23 September), declining to draft on command as sycophancy (23 September), and bounding a statistical claim (29 September).
- **Where the spin is:** Grok found it in captions, not in what the AIs said: 2 material, 4 mild, 22 none across 28 posts. It found the on-screen AIs mostly bounding their own claims.
