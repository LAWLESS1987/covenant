# Wide universe, mixed and matched. 2026-09-07

**Asked:** *"not just my assets look at all of them"*, then *"mix and match find
fluid workable repeatable patterns"*. Both were built and run.

---

## 0. Two facts about the universe before any strategy

Of the **top 170 OKX spot USDT pairs by 24-hour volume**, only **33 have daily
history reaching back to February 2021.** Eighty percent of today's most liquid
crypto **did not exist five years ago.**

And the 33 are all *survivors*. Every coin that went to zero in that window —
LUNA/UST, FTT, and hundreds of smaller ones — is absent from this data by
construction, because it is not listed today. **Every number below is inflated
by survivorship, and the strategy most inflated by it is buy-and-hold**, since
that is the one with full exposure to the winners that remained. The true gap
between holding and the rules is narrower than what follows. It is not narrow
enough to change the conclusion.

## 1. What "mix and match" and "fluid" actually produced

Every approach run against the **same shuffled-returns control** (same assets,
same volatility, same drift, timing signal destroyed).

| approach | 5-yr total | on shuffled data | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BUY & HOLD** | **+287.7%** | +246.5% | +41 |
| best solo variant (L10/50) | +267.1% | +74.8% | +192 |
| MIX — average of all 18 variants | +99.5% | +25.3% | +74 |
| MIX — vote ≥ 0.5 | +96.6% | +33.4% | +63 |
| MIX — vote ≥ 0.7 | −2.3% | −82.7% | +80 |
| **FLUID — pick best of trailing 60d** | **+82.5%** | −25.3% | +108 |
| **FLUID — pick best of trailing 120d** | **+61.7%** | −5.4% | +67 |
| XSECT — long top quintile, 90d momentum | −4.1% | −56.5% | +52 |

**Nothing mixed, matched, voted or adapted came close to doing nothing.**
Holding returned +287.7%. The best combination returned +99.5% — about a third
of it. Cross-sectional momentum, the one family that genuinely needs the wider
universe, **lost money**.

## 2. "Fluid" specifically backfired, and it is worth knowing why

Adaptive switching — each day, run whichever variant did best over the trailing
window — returned **+82.5%**, *below the simple average of the same variants*
(+99.5%) and far below the best single one (+267.1%). Its 2022 was **−68.4%**.

That is the mechanism failing exactly as expected: picking the recently-best
rule is momentum-of-strategies, and it buys each rule after its good run and
sells it after its bad one. Adaptivity did not add skill; it added turnover and
whipsaw. "Fluid" is where overfitting hides best, and here it did not even hide
— it just cost money.

## 3. The one real finding, stated carefully

The **breakout** family separates from noise by a wide margin:

| | real | shuffled | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| breakout 20d | +29.1% | −131.1% | **+160** |
| breakout 55d | +46.9% | −87.6% | **+134** |
| breakout 100d | +57.4% | −66.0% | **+123** |

On shuffled data breakout *loses badly*, because new highs do not persist in
noise. On real data it does not. **That is genuine short-horizon momentum, and
it is the only thing in this project's history that has cleanly separated from
its own null.**

And it is still not worth trading: **+57.4% over five years against +287.7% for
holding.** It is in the market a fraction of the time, so it captures a fraction
of the drift. The honest description is the same one `d2_regime_deep.py` reached
on the 200-day rule — **risk control, not a return edge.** Real, and not what
you want it to be.

*A note on why buy-and-hold's "gap" is small (+41).* Shuffling destroys the
**order** of returns, not their **drift**. A rule that is always long earns the
same drift in shuffled data, so the null for buy-and-hold is buy-and-hold. The
shuffle tests for **timing skill**, and buy-and-hold makes no timing claim. Its
+287.7% is market exposure, not skill — and it is the number every rule here
has to beat, and none does.

## 4. What this settles

- **Breadth did not rescue Group A.** 33 assets instead of 8, six years, every
  combination structure — the ranking is unchanged and holding still wins.
- **Mixing does not manufacture edge.** An average of eighteen weak rules is a
  weaker rule with less variance. Voting thresholds made it worse.
- **Adaptive switching is a cost, not a feature**, unless the components have
  real and *persistent* differences to switch between. These do not.
- **Momentum is real and is priced.** Breakout beats its null decisively and
  still loses to holding by 5×. That is what an edge too small to pay for
  looks like from the inside.

The methodology stands as the deliverable: shuffled-returns control on every
candidate, independence correction, multiple-comparison bar, cost of harvesting.
Four checks. Nothing has passed all four yet.
